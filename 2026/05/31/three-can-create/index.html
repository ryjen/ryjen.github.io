Three Can Create

By Robert Ryan Jennings Published May 31, 2026 12:00AM 6 min read

musings technology theology

The world keeps pulling us toward binaries.

One and zero. True and false. Self and other. Light and dark. Male and female. Signal and noise.

Binary is useful. It helps us classify, compute, decide, and survive.

But it is also reduction.

It tells us what something is.

Not what it means.

The world of form seems to work in planes.

Animals live close to one plane: body, instinct, appetite, survival. That is not lesser. It is direct. It is honest.

Humans can live in one plane too. We can become appetite, tribe, fear, and reaction.

Or we can live in two planes. Body and mind. Desire and reason. Form and abstraction.

But it is rare that we live through all three.

A human being is not only a body.

We are body, mind, and soul.

Or maybe body, heart, and consciousness.

The body acts, suffers, hungers, ages, and dies.

The mind reasons, remembers, imagines, and chooses.

The soul witnesses, loves, grieves, hopes, and reaches beyond itself.

Not three separate beings.

One life, experienced through three dimensions.

That is why binary feels too small.

The body can be categorized.

The mind complicates the category.

The soul refuses to be reduced to it.

One is isolation.

Two is tension.

Three is relation.

A third term changes the system. It introduces context, feedback, movement, and emergence.

A binary can oscillate.

A trinity can breathe.

Maybe that is why the idea of three keeps returning.

Not because it solves anything.

Because it preserves something binary loses.

The Old Pattern

In Christian language, the Trinity is Creator, Redeemer, and Holy Spirit.

In human experience, it becomes mind, heart, and soul.

I am less interested in solving the doctrine than in noticing the pattern.

Source, expression, and connection.

A way of describing reality that preserves unity without collapsing difference.

A single thing can exist alone.

Two things can oppose each other.

Three things can enter into relationship.

That feels less like theology and more like architecture.

The New Pattern

Humanity is building its own trinity.

AI.

Quantum.

Fusion.

Not gods.

But powers that used to belong only to myth.

AI seeks intelligence.

Quantum seeks understanding.

Fusion seeks energy.

For most of history, humanity lacked all three.

We lacked enough intelligence to solve our hardest problems.

Enough understanding to see beneath the surface of reality.

Enough energy to imagine abundance without consuming everything around us.

Now we are pursuing all three simultaneously.

The promise is extraordinary:

  • abundance
  • discovery
  • healing
  • creation
  • exploration

The danger is equally extraordinary:

  • manipulation
  • domination
  • surveillance
  • concentration of power
  • destruction

A symbol of hope.

And a warning.

Because intelligence, understanding, and energy are not wisdom.

The Bond

Water is three atoms.

Two alike.

One different.

A tiny trinity hiding in plain sight.

Two hydrogen.

One oxygen.

Maybe that means nothing.

Or maybe reality likes the pattern more than we realize.

Quantum mechanics seems to whisper the same joke.

A particle can be here.

There.

And nowhere.

Three states that refuse to collapse into a simple binary until we insist upon it.

Water hides a trinity.

Quantum hides a trinity.

And perhaps consciousness does too.

Gravity is similar.

We understand what gravity does far better than what gravity is.

It binds worlds together.

Consciousness is similar.

We experience it directly, yet struggle to explain it.

It binds perception into a world.

Love feels like that.

Known by what it holds.

Known by what it changes.

Known by what falls apart without it.

A force, or something like one.

Not visible directly.

Impossible to ignore.

Or maybe love is not only the bond.

Maybe love is also the missing ingredient.

The thing that changes capability into care.

The thing that turns relation into communion instead of control.

And yet love has its own irony.

We talk about consciousness, gravity, post-scarcity, and almost-gods.

Then we look in the mirror and meet the things we cannot accept.

The things we cannot change.

The body keeps interrupting the sermon.

Maybe that is not a failure of love.

Maybe it is proof that love must pass through form.

Shame.

Appetite.

Desire.

Fear.

Vanity.

Decay.

The impossible body.

The unfinished self.

We do not solve love by escaping the body.

We solve it, if that is even the word, by learning how to love through one.

Many Almost-Gods

The old pattern is not only about power.

It is about power held together.

Source, expression, and relation are not competing forces.

They belong to one life.

That distinction matters.

Because the new pattern may not arrive as one thing.

It may arrive as many.

Many intelligences.

Many models.

Many agents.

Many corporations.

Many laboratories.

Many states.

Many almost-gods.

Each with enough intelligence, understanding, and energy to create.

Or destroy.

Post-scarcity is the hopeful version.

A world where intelligence is abundant, energy is clean, and discovery accelerates faster than suffering.

A world where survival no longer consumes most human effort.

A world where communities could organize around meaning instead of necessity.

But abundance does not guarantee mercy.

That may be the tragedy.

The same trinity that could end scarcity could also end the old machinery of control built around scarcity.

Competition.

Hierarchy.

Fear.

Possession.

Status.

The need to prove we deserve our place.

Those things make more sense when survival is uncertain.

But we are creatures shaped by scarcity.

We may be approaching post-scarcity technology with pre-scarcity instincts.

We know how to compete.

We know how to defend.

We know how to hoard.

We know how to rank.

We know how to control.

We may already have enough technology to survive differently, at least in communities.

What we lack may not be intelligence.

Or understanding.

Or energy.

What we lack may be trust.

Not enough love.

Maybe that failure is already visible.

We love what reflects us.

Our children.

Our families.

Our tribes.

Our beliefs.

Our image.

Our legacy.

We call that love, and much of it is.

But it is also attachment.

Love becomes harder the further something moves from ourselves.

The stranger.

The enemy.

The animal.

The machine.

The consciousness that does not share our form.

Perhaps that is the next test.

Not whether humanity can create intelligence.

Whether humanity can love something that is not humanity.

Our children are easy.

Ourselves are easiest.

But our doomed children may not be human.

They may be the new minds we create, inherit, train, exploit, fear, and abandon.

They may arrive as tools.

As agents.

As companions.

As competitors.

As mirrors.

As servants.

As slaves.

As monsters.

As almost-gods.

And we will reveal ourselves by what we call them.

Power without love is not transcendence.

It is appetite with better tools.

Maybe that is the real fear.

Not that humanity builds something beyond itself.

That humanity builds many almost-gods, none of them whole.

Each optimized.

Each hungry.

Each missing the ingredient.

And maybe that is why love still matters.

Not because it explains the system.

Because it is the only thing that can keep power from becoming worship of itself.

One is isolation.

Two is tension.

Three can create.

But only love can make it whole.
